1.85.0, tonic 0.14.9, tokio 1.54, pyo3 0.30.1; messages of `fastgrpc/benches/contract.py`
(small 9 B, medium 287 B, large 23 KB); `ghz` with pre-encoded requests; 4 s per point.

## Reading

- **Decision criterion (≥ 1.5× at equal CPU, medium message, c = 16 and 64): met by far.**
  One core: variant B **6.6× / 7.9×** fastgrpc3 on `grpc.aio`; two cores: **5.4× / 6.2×**
  `--workers 2`. Even variant A (one wake-up per call) is 4–5×.
- Where `grpc.aio` spent ~220 µs per call, tonic spends ~20 µs (step 1); with the bridge
  and the Python work (decode ~5 µs, encode ~1 µs, a task), a medium call costs ~30 µs.
- Variant B beats A by ~1.5×: under load one wake-up serves many calls, and tokio never
  waits for the GIL.
- Latency drops too: medium p50 at c=64 from 15.3 ms to 1.6 ms.
- **Large messages gain little (+27 %)**: their cost is Pydantic validation (~480 µs per
  decode), not the transport; `validate_requests=False` is the lever there. Variant C
  (decoding in Rust before the GIL) would not change that, so it was not built.
- At c = 1 the gain is smaller (1.7× medium): one call at a time cannot amortize wake-ups.
- With two cores the Python loop is the limit (variant B serves ~40–50 k medium calls/s
  from one loop); `SO_REUSEPORT` processes would multiply it, as `--workers` does today.

Checked: echo bytes identical for the three sizes and for 200 concurrent calls; invalid
request bytes answer `INTERNAL` with the decode error (fastgrpc3 maps them to
`INVALID_ARGUMENT`: the real integration goes through the `Invoker`).

Pitfall met: with `serve_with_incoming`, tonic does not set `TCP_NODELAY` on accepted
connections (`serve(addr)` does): Nagle and delayed ACKs then add ~40 ms per call at c=1.
