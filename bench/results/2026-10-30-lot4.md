# Lot 4 results — fastgrpc3 on tonic, 30/10/2026

Same machine and method as `2026-10-30.md`, but the server is now the real fastgrpc3 app
(`fastgrpc/benches/e2e_bench.py --modes fastgrpc3`: `Invoker`, Pydantic validation,
`MessageCodec`), run on each transport with `FASTGRPC_TRANSPORT`. Server on one core, `ghz`
on 2-7, 4 s per point, two interleaved passes.

## Unary, requests/s

| transport | small c=1 | small c=16 | small c=64 | medium c=1 | medium c=16 | medium c=64 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `grpc_aio` | 4,034–4,115 | 4,273–4,348 | 4,257–4,263 | 3,523–3,558 | 3,914–4,043 | 3,922–3,941 |
| `tonic` | 7,941–8,008 | 34,476–34,827 | 44,324–44,395 | 5,738–5,837 | 23,812–23,908 | 29,896–30,509 |

fastgrpc3 on tonic: **8.0× / 10.4×** `grpc.aio` on the small message at c=16 / c=64,
**6.0× / 7.7×** on the medium one, ~2× at c=1. Close to the bare variant B of lot 2
(medium 24,370–25,957 / 30,955–31,897): the shared `Invoker` costs little.

## After lot 5 (cancellation and deadlines)

Same session: fastgrpc3 on tonic, small c=1/16/64 7,661–7,728 / 34,093–34,321 /
43,398–43,520, medium 5,663–5,731 / 24,190–24,649 / 29,919–29,976 requests/s: within
1–3 % of lot 4 (`ghz` sends `grpc-timeout`, so every call now arms a deadline timer).

Client stream of 200,000 messages of 1 KB into a fastgrpc3 handler on tonic (server in its
own process): server RSS 50.8 → 51.0 MiB with a fast handler, 50.7 → 51.0 MiB with one that
sleeps 1 ms every 100 messages; 5.5 s, the Python client being the bottleneck.
